# Theoretical Computer Science — Independent Study

A self-directed study based on lecture materials from Saarland University's **Introduction to Theoretical Computer Science** course.

The goal of this project is to develop a deeper understanding of the mathematical foundations of computation, formal reasoning, and the theoretical limits of computational models through structured notes, worked exercises, and proof-oriented learning.

## Scope

The study follows the subject areas outlined in the course's module handbook.

- **Formal Languages and Automata:** The Chomsky hierarchy, grammars, automata, closure properties, and pumping lemmas.
- **Models of Computation:** Determinism, nondeterminism, Turing machines, and equivalent models of general computation, including μ-recursive functions and random access machines.
- **Computability and Decidability:** Computability, non-computability, decidability, undecidability, and reducibility.
- **Complexity Theory:** Time and space complexity, resource bounds, and the complexity classes P and NP.
- **NP-Completeness:** The fundamental concepts and techniques underlying NP-completeness.

## Repository Contents

This repository serves as a working record of the study and include:

- **Lecture Notes:** Structured explanations of definitions, theorems, and key concepts.
- **Exercise Solutions:** Worked solutions with reasoning and mathematical justification.
- **Proofs and Examples:** Step-by-step explorations of theoretical results and illustrative cases.

## Learning Objectives

Through this independent study, I aim to strengthen my ability to:

- Reason rigorously about mathematical definitions, relations, and proofs.
- Understand different models of computation and their capabilities.
- Analyze whether computational problems are decidable or undecidable.
- Relate computational problems through reductions.
- Understand the theoretical foundations of time and space complexity.
- Explain concepts through clear technical notes and independently worked exercises.
